# Analysis of 10 Review Articles

**Author:** Ayazhan Orynbasar
**Part of:** [Literature Review](03-literature-review.md), Section 8

This document summarizes the ten review papers selected for the literature review. For each review it states the scope, the trends and themes, the research gaps, the frequently used approaches, and how the review shapes the understanding of the research problem. The companion document [03a-original-research-articles.md](03a-original-research-articles.md) covers the ten original research studies.

---

## 1. Cannas and Gosling (2021)

**Article:** A decade of engineering-to-order (2010–2020): Progress and emerging themes (Cannas and Gosling, 2021).

**Scope.** This systematic review covers research on engineer-to-order manufacturing published during 2010–2020.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** Major themes include ETO definitions and decoupling, engineering and production-flow integration, supply-chain integration, planning and control, uncertainty and risk, Industry 4.0, design automation, engineering management, and lean approaches.

**Research gaps/future directions.** The review identifies future needs including more positive science, comparative research, multidisciplinary research, and prescriptive approaches.

**Frequently used approaches.** The reviewed ETO literature uses a mixture of conceptual research, empirical studies, planning and control approaches, process integration, and Industry 4.0 technologies.

**How it shapes understanding.** The review establishes ETO as a broad, cross-functional problem rather than a narrow production-scheduling problem and therefore provides a foundation for integrating customer, engineering, supply-chain, and production activities in the proposed system.

---

## 2. Serrano-Ruiz, Mula, and Poler (2021)

**Article:** Smart manufacturing scheduling: A literature review (Serrano-Ruiz et al., 2021).

**Scope.** The review analyzes 68 scientific publications on smart manufacturing scheduling.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** The literature is organized around scheduling with Digital Twins and Zero-Defect Manufacturing, scheduling with Industry 4.0 technologies, and intelligent or autonomous scheduling.

**Research gaps/future directions.** The review identifies the continued need for more autonomous scheduling and stronger integration of real-time disturbance response.

**Frequently used approaches.** Digital Twins, simulation, optimization, intelligent algorithms, real-time data, and autonomous or adaptive decision-making are frequently used.

**How it shapes understanding.** The review shows why a showroom-to-factory system should not stop at order creation: production decisions must be able to react to real factory conditions and disturbances.

---

## 3. Schlemitz and Mezhuyev (2024)

**Article:** Approaches for data collection and process standardization in smart manufacturing: Systematic literature review (Schlemitz and Mezhuyev, 2024).

**Scope.** The review examines data collection and process standardization in smart manufacturing, with attention to standards, OPC UA, information models, and interoperability.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** Major themes include industrial standards, process integration, architecture, interoperability, data engineering, OPC UA, Digital Twins, and ontologies. RAMI 4.0 is a recurring architectural reference.

**Research gaps/future directions.** A major challenge is interoperability between heterogeneous and legacy systems. The review indicates the need for more consistent data models and standardization approaches.

**Frequently used approaches.** Industrial standards, OPC UA, information modelling, architecture frameworks, Digital Twins, and interoperability mechanisms are prominent.

**How it shapes understanding.** The review clarifies that the proposed order-management system needs a standardized data layer so that showroom, ERP/WMS, APS, MES, and shopfloor systems can exchange consistent order information.

---

## 4. Eichenseer and Winkler (2024)

**Article:** A data-oriented shopfloor management in the production context: a systematic literature review (Eichenseer and Winkler, 2024).

**Scope.** The review examines data-oriented shopfloor management through Technology, Organisation, and People dimensions and analyzes 79 publications.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** The literature emphasizes the transformation from data to information, decisions, and feedback, while technology receives more attention than organizational and human dimensions.

**Research gaps/future directions.** The review identifies a lack of holistic approaches that address heterogeneous production environments and balance technology with organization and people.

**Frequently used approaches.** Data collection, information systems, digital production technologies, organizational approaches, and decision-support mechanisms are common.

**How it shapes understanding.** The review demonstrates that an intelligent order-management system cannot be evaluated only as software. Roles, responsibilities, processes, and user interaction must also be part of the system design.

---

## 5. Li, Zhao, and Yang (2023)

**Article:** Research on the Application Status of Machine Vision Technology in Furniture Manufacturing Process (Li et al., 2023).

**Scope.** This systematic review examines 128 publications on machine vision applications in furniture manufacturing from 2011–2022.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** The literature is classified into information collection, quality detection, positioning and automatic sorting, and intelligent monitoring. Quality detection is the largest application category in the reviewed literature.

**Research gaps/future directions.** The review identifies the need for stronger integration with deep learning, three-dimensional point-cloud methods, and broader intelligent furniture-manufacturing applications.

**Frequently used approaches.** Image processing, machine learning, deep learning, 3D vision, and barcode or QR-code technologies are frequently used.

**How it shapes understanding.** The review shows how automated identification and inspection can supply production-status data to a broader digital manufacturing system, supporting the feedback component of the proposed architecture.

---

## 6. Harfeldt-Berg and Olhager (2024)

**Article:** The customer order decoupling point in empirical operations and supply chain management research: a systematic literature review and framework (Harfeldt-Berg and Olhager, 2024).

**Scope.** The review examines empirical research on the customer order decoupling point and analyzes 40 empirical articles and 32 relevant factors.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** The factors are grouped into market and product characteristics, operations, supply chain, and performance. The framework distinguishes configurations in which manufacturing is positioned upstream or downstream of the customer order.

**Research gaps/future directions.** The review indicates the need to understand how different CODP configurations interact with operational and supply-chain conditions.

**Frequently used approaches.** Empirical operations and supply-chain studies are the main methodological basis, with frameworks used to organize CODP-related factors.

**How it shapes understanding.** The review helps define where customer orders influence production decisions and supports the proposed system’s focus on connecting customer commitments with operational capabilities.

---

## 7. Naldi et al. (2025)

**Article:** Unlocking the Potential of Mass Customization Through Industry 4.0: Mapping Research Streams and Future Directions (Naldi et al., 2025).

**Scope.** The systematic review covers research on mass customization and Industry 4.0 from 2011–2024 and analyzes 3441 publications using a PRISMA-based process.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** Three main streams are identified: conceptual frameworks and performance, enabling technologies and methods across the product lifecycle, and supply-chain strategies.

**Research gaps/future directions.** The review emphasizes integration complexity and the need to combine customization, enabling technologies, supply-chain management, and customer involvement more effectively.

**Frequently used approaches.** Product modularity, customer co-design platforms, reconfigurable production, real-time data, artificial intelligence, predictive analytics, and Digital Twins are prominent.

**How it shapes understanding.** The review provides a broad technology and organizational foundation for the proposed intelligent order-management system and supports treating the showroom and factory as connected parts of one customization process.

---

## 8. Bouchard, Gamache, and Abdulnour (2023)

**Article:** Operationalizing Mass Customization in Manufacturing SMEs—A Systematic Literature Review (Bouchard et al., 2023).

**Scope.** This PRISMA-based review examines the operationalization of mass customization in manufacturing SMEs and analyzes 49 publications in its main analysis.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** Six major factors are identified: product modularity, process modularity, business agility, technology use, digital supply chain, and collaboration networks.

**Research gaps/future directions.** The review identifies insufficient guidance on implementation priorities, maturity, and concrete tools, suggesting a need for clearer implementation sequences.

**Frequently used approaches.** Research frequently addresses modular product and process design, digital technologies, supply-chain integration, agility, and collaboration.

**How it shapes understanding.** The review highlights that a configurator or order-management application should be integrated with product architecture, processes, supply chains, and organizational collaboration rather than implemented as an isolated tool.

---

## 9. Abdel-Aty and Negri (2024)

**Article:** Conceptualizing the digital thread for smart manufacturing: a systematic literature review (Abdel-Aty and Negri, 2024).

**Scope.** The review investigates the Digital Thread across the product lifecycle from design and engineering through production, maintenance, use, and end of life. It combines bibliometric analysis with deeper content analysis.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** Major themes include data integration, traceability, information sharing, interoperability, and lifecycle connectivity. Technologies include IoT, cloud computing, big data, AI/ML, Digital Twins, and data modelling.

**Research gaps/future directions.** The review identifies continuing problems with standardization, empirical implementation, security, and safety.

**Frequently used approaches.** Digital Thread architectures, IoT, cloud systems, Digital Twins, AI/ML, data models, and interoperability mechanisms are common.

**How it shapes understanding.** The Digital Thread concept supports the idea that the customer order should remain traceable as a common information object from showroom requirements through configuration, planning, execution, and feedback.

---

## 10. Bhalla, Alfnes, and Hvolby (2023)

**Article:** Tools and practices for tactical delivery date setting in engineer-to-order environments: a systematic literature review (Bhalla et al., 2023).

**Scope.** The review examines tactical delivery-date setting in ETO environments and evaluates research on tools and practices used to estimate and commit delivery dates.

**Trends, themes, and classifications.** Four major areas are identified: cross-functional coordination, supply-chain coordination, process formalization, and planning or decision-support tools.

**Research gaps/future directions.** The review finds that existing tools often concentrate on tactical capacity and production lead time, while coordination and complete order-specific lead-time estimation receive less attention.

**Frequently used approaches.** Capacity planning, production planning, decision-support tools, formalized processes, and cross-functional coordination are recurring approaches.

**How it shapes understanding.** The review demonstrates that a showroom delivery promise should account for the full order lifecycle, including engineering, procurement, production, and other order-specific activities, rather than relying on factory production time alone.
